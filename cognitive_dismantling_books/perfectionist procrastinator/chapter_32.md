# Chapter 32: This Way to Stop

We have reached the chapter that the whole book has been building toward. Now I am going to tell you, plainly and without elaboration, how to stop being a perfectionist.

This way to Stop is not a technique. It is not a system. It is a shift in perception, and the shift is this:

**You do not have to make your work perfect. You have to make it real.**

Everything else in this book has been clearing the ground for that single sentence. The fear of judgment, the black shadows, the flawless standard. They have all been telling you a lie, and the lie is that your work must be perfect before it can exist. The truth is the opposite. Your work exists so that it can be imperfect. It exists to be released, seen, judged, and improved. The imperfection is not a failure of the work. It is the work's condition of being real.

Let me show you what this shift actually changes. It changes nothing about your skill. It changes nothing about your taste. What it changes is the demand. You are no longer required to produce a flawless thing. You are required to produce a real thing. And a real thing is finite, flawed, and releasable. The demand has shrunk from the impossible to the merely difficult, and the merely difficult is something a human being can do.

Once the demand is corrected, everything else follows. The dread loosens, because the release is no longer a verdict on your worth. The polishing loses its purpose, because there is no flawless version to wait for. The "just one more check" loses its power, because the check was serving the old demand. You are not fighting the habits anymore. The habits are starving, because the belief that fed them has been removed.

This is why I have said, again and again, that you must not try to stop polishing with willpower. Willpower fights the behavior while the belief is still alive, and the belief wins. This way removes the belief, and the behavior dies of its own accord, the way a plant dies when the water stops. You will not have to force yourself to stop polishing. You will stop because polishing no longer makes sense.

Now let me give you the practical shape of the shift, so you know what it looks like in a working day.

You receive a task. You do it. You do not ask whether it is perfect. You ask whether it is done. When it is done, you release it. That is the whole method.

There is no hidden step. There is no "and then you may polish if you have extra time." That last clause is the trap, and you must not include it. Extra time is not a license to polish; it is a license to ship more. The person who finishes the report in four hours and has four more hours does not spend the four hours polishing the report. They ship the report and start the next thing. That is how bodies of work are built.

I want to be honest with you about the difficulty, because you deserve the truth. The shift is easy to understand and hard to live, at first. The old belief will not dissolve overnight. The urge to polish will return, especially when you are tired, or when the work matters. This is not a sign that the method has failed. It is the withdrawal period, and it passes. You will be tempted, many times, to reach for the old demand. Each time, if you refuse the relief, the belief grows a little weaker, until one day you realize that you have shipped a dozen things and you did not feel the fear once.

Here is the last thing I want you to understand. This way is not about lowering your standards. It is about putting the standard in its proper place. The standard is the eye that knows good work. It is not the hand that prevents the release. You will keep your eye. You will keep your taste. What you will lose is the demand that the work be flawless before it can exist. And you will find, to your surprise, that the work is better without the demand. Not because the demand improved it, it never did, but because the work finally gets made, and made work is better than perfect work, because perfect work does not exist.

**EXERCISE: The Shift**

Put the book down. Take your paper. Write the old demand, the one you have been living under: *"My work must be perfect before it can be released."*

Now write the new demand, in your own words: *"My work must be real. It must be released. It will be flawed, and that is the condition of its existence."*

Read them both. Notice which one has been making you miserable. Notice which one is actually possible.

Now, for the rest of this book, whenever you feel the urge to polish, stop and say the new demand out loud.

You are not giving up quality. You are giving up a lie. The lie has been running your life. The truth is lighter. Hold on to it. We are almost done.

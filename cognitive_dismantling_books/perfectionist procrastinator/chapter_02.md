# Chapter 2: The Easy Method

The object of this book is to get you into the frame of mind in which, instead of the normal method of "being more disciplined," you start right away with a feeling of elation, as if you had been cured of a terrible disease. From then on, the more you go through life, the more you will look at your work and wonder how you ever feared it in the first place.

Provided that you are not already "cured," it is essential to keep polishing until you have finished the book completely. This may appear to be a contradiction. Later I shall be explaining that polishing does absolutely nothing for you at all. In fact, one of the many conundrums about perfectionism is that when we are actually doing it—re-reading the same sentence, restarting the same project—we look at ourselves and wonder why we are doing it. It is only when we are forced to consider shipping the work that the "polish" becomes precious. So do not attempt to "let go" before you have finished the whole book. As you read further, your desire to polish will gradually be reduced. Do not go off half-cocked; this could be fatal. Remember, all you have to do is to follow the instructions.

With the benefit of years of feedback, this instruction has caused me more frustration than any other. When the book was first printed, I gave copies to perfectionists who were still drowning in drafts. I was surprised and hurt to learn that, months later, they hadn't bothered to finish the book. I eventually discovered that they didn't finish it because they were *waiting for the perfect reading conditions before they started*.

This is the nature of the beast. But what have you got to lose? If you don't stop polishing at the end of the book, you are no worse off than you are now. YOU HAVE ABSOLUTELY NOTHING TO LOSE AND SO MUCH TO GAIN!

Basically my method is the complete opposite of the normal method of trying to "loosen up." The normal method is to list the disadvantages of perfectionism and say, "If only I can force myself to be less fussy, eventually the desire to perfect will go." This is the logical way to go about it, but it is difficult to succeed using this method for the following reasons:

1.  **Stopping isn't the problem.** Every time you actually ship something, you stop perfecting. The real problem is day two, day ten, or day ten thousand, when in a moment of fatigue you think, "Let me just improve this one more thing," and suddenly you are a perfectionist again.
2.  **The praise scares should stop us.** They make it harder. Tell a perfectionist that their standards are ruining their life, and the first thing they will do is double down on the next draft. The more pressure you apply, the more they need their "safety" mechanism—which is *polishing*.
3.  **The "excellence" illusion.** All reasons for "loosening up" create a sense of sacrifice. We feel we are being forced to give up our "quality" and become sloppy, careless people.

The Easy Method is basically this: initially to forget the reasons we'd like to stop, to face the perfectionism problem, and to ask ourselves the following questions:

1.  What is it doing *for* me?
2.  Do I actually enjoy the feeling of never being satisfied?
3.  Do I really need to go through life with this heavy weight on my chest just to avoid shipping a result that might have a flaw?

The beautiful truth is that it does absolutely nothing for you at all. I do not mean that the disadvantages outweigh the advantages; all perfectionists know that. I mean there are *not any advantages* from perfectionism. The only advantage it ever had was a temporary illusion of relief from judgment.

Most perfectionists find it necessary to rationalize why they polish, but the reasons are all fallacies and illusions. The first thing we are going to do is to remove these fallacies and illusions. You will realize that there is nothing to give up. Not only is there nothing to give up but there are marvelous, positive gains from finishing things. Once the illusion that life will never be quite as "good" without the standards is removed, then we can go back to reconsider the wasted hours and the missed opportunities. These realizations will become positive additional aids to help you achieve what you really desire—to enjoy the whole of your life free from the slavery of the standard.

**EXERCISE: The Illusion of Quality**

Put the book down. Think of a project you are currently "polishing"—a message you keep rewriting before you send it. Your brain tells you that by not sending it, you are protecting your reputation. But are you relaxed? Close your eyes and check. Is there a tiny, nagging voice in your head? Now imagine you had already sent it. Which version of you is actually free?

The "quality" of perfectionism is a lie. It is a prison with invisible bars.

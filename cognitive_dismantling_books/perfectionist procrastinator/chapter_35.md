# Chapter 35: Will it be Harder for Me?

There is an objection that every perfectionist saves for the moment when they are about to change. It is the last defense, the final wall, and it is almost always voiced in the same words.

*"This is all very well for you. But it will be harder for me."*

The perfectionist has a list of reasons why they are the exception. The list is long and it is dear to them. It includes things like:

"My work is judged more harshly than anyone else's."
"I have higher standards than most people—my eye sees flaws they can't even see."
"I've been this way my whole life. It's who I am."
"I work in a field where quality is everything."
"I'm more sensitive to criticism than other people."

Let me take these one by one, because each one is the fear, speaking through a megaphone, and each one will crumble the moment you examine it.

**"My work is judged more harshly."** Is it? Or is your *fear* of judgment harsher? Think about the actual verdicts you have received in your career. The criticisms, the rejections. They are real, and some of them stung. But compare them to the verdicts you have *imagined*—the ones that kept you polishing, that kept you from shipping. The imagined verdicts are worse. They are also more numerous. The real world has been gentler than your imagination, and your imagination is the thing doing the judging.

**"I have higher standards. My eye sees flaws others can't."** This is true, and it is precisely the problem. The sharp eye is not a gift that exempts you from the method. It is the tool the trap has been using. Of course you see the flaws. Everyone with taste sees flaws. The question is what you do with the sight. The free worker sees the flaws too, and ships anyway. Your eye is not the reason it will be harder for you. Your eye is the reason you have been polishing.

**"I've been this way my whole life. It's who I am."** The most dangerous sentence in the language. You are not a perfectionist the way a stone is a stone. You are a person with a habit, and the habit was installed, and habits can be uninstalled. You were not born with the fear of judgment. You were taught it, over years, by the gold stars and the critiques. What was taught can be unlearned. It will not be easy the first week. It will be easier the second. "It's who I am" is the fear's final argument, and it is the weakest one.

**"My field demands quality."** Every field demands quality. The surgeon, the pilot, the architect—these are fields where quality is not a preference but a necessity, and even they do not polish forever. They check, they verify, and then they release, because the release is the whole point. The difference between you and them is not your field. It is your relationship with release.

**"I'm more sensitive to criticism."** You may be. Sensitivity is real, and it is not a weakness. But the method does not ask you to become insensitive. It asks you to stop pre-empting the criticism. The sensitivity that makes you feel the judgment keenly is the same sensitivity that makes you protect the work until it is beyond judgment—which means forever. Your sensitivity is not a reason to keep polishing. It is a reason to stop.

Now, here is the truth that answers all of these at once. You are not special in the way you fear. Every perfectionist who has ever been freed believed, at this point in the book, that the method would not work for them. Every single one. And every single one was wrong. Not because they were not sensitive, not because their work was not judged, not because their standards were not high. They were wrong because the trap is not sensitive to your circumstances. It is sensitive to your belief.

So here is my answer to the objection. Yes, it will be hard. It was hard for everyone. The first releases will be uncomfortable. The withdrawal will visit you. You will want to believe, in those moments, that you are the exception.

Believe me instead. I have seen the trap in its strongest form—I lived in it. And I have seen it fall in the strongest cases. The trap falls the same way every time: not when the perfectionist becomes stronger, but when they see that it was never strength they needed. It was clarity.

You are not harder to free than anyone else. You are the same as everyone else, which is to say: entirely freeable.

**EXERCISE: The Objection Sheet**

Put the book down. Take your paper. Write down every reason you believe it will be harder for you. The field, the history, the sensitivity, the standards. All of them. Spare nothing.

Now, for each reason, write the counter-evidence. The real verdicts you have received, and how mild they were compared to the imagined ones.

Finally, write the sentence that closes the case: *"The trap always tells me it will be harder for me. The trap is always lying. I am not the exception. I am the rule, and the rule is that freedom is available."*

Then ship the next thing, and let the trap's argument die of evidence.

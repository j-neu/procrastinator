# Chapter 28: Timing

There is a question every perfectionist asks, sooner or later. It is the question that has delayed more change than any other. And it is a trap.

The question is: *"When is the right time to stop?"*

The perfectionist does not ask it idly. They ask it because they have convinced themselves that the change requires the right conditions. "I'll stop being so perfectionistic when this project is done." "When I have more time, I'll be less fussy." "When things calm down, I'll finally relax."

And because the conditions are never right, the change never happens.

Here is the truth about the timing question: **the right time is now. There is no other time.**

Let me show you why the perfectionist's answer to "when" is always wrong.

The perfectionist says: "I'll change when this project is done." But the project will never be done, because the perfectionist's standard prevents completion. They have set the condition for change at a point they are structurally incapable of reaching.

The perfectionist says: "I'll change when I have more time." But time is not the issue. The issue is the fear. You could give the perfectionist a month of uninterrupted time, and they would spend it polishing. The polish expands to fill whatever time is available, and then demands more.

The perfectionist says: "I'll change when I'm in a better place." But the better place is a mirage. You have been waiting for it for years. The condition that keeps you from it is the condition you are waiting to fix before you fix it.

This is the great catch-22 of the perfectionist's life. The change is the cause of the better place, not the effect of it. You will not relax first and then stop polishing. You will stop polishing, and then (days, weeks later), you will discover that you can relax. You will not feel confident first and then ship. You will ship, and the shipping will produce the confidence. The sequence is backwards from what you have been assuming your entire life.

There is a reason for this. The perfectionist's confidence is not the source of their behavior; it is the result of it. The person who ships repeatedly builds confidence on the evidence of the shipping. The person who waits for confidence before shipping is waiting for a feeling they have never produced. You cannot manufacture the feeling without the behavior. The behavior comes first. The feeling follows.

So let me answer the timing question directly. The right time to stop polishing is before this project. The right time to start shipping is this afternoon. The right time to release the draft is the next time your hand reaches for the "just one more check." The right time to tell the truth to one person is this week.

Not when you feel ready. Not when the conditions are right. The moment you stop demanding perfect conditions for the change is the moment the change becomes possible. The change does not require good conditions. It requires a decision, and the decision requires only this: that you accept the timing will never be comfortable, and proceed anyway.

I am going to make a confession, and I want you to hear it. I have never met a perfectionist who was ready to change. Every single one of them has made their change at a moment when they felt least ready, tired, fearful, convinced it would fail. And it worked, not because they were ready, but because they stopped waiting to be ready. The readiness is a myth that the trap uses to keep you inside. The trap cannot survive a decision. It is designed to survive waiting.

So do not ask me when. Ask me how. And the how is simple: you stop negotiating with the fear. You ship the next thing, whatever it is, however unready you feel. You do it now, because now is the only time that exists.

The right time to be free is the same for every perfectionist who has ever lived. It is the moment they decide that the finished work matters more than the flawless work, and that the flawless work was never going to exist anyway.

That moment is now. It has always been now. The only question that has ever mattered is whether you will take it.

**EXERCISE: The Ready Test**

Put the book down. Take your paper. Write down the condition you have been waiting for. The "I'll change when..." sentence. Fill in the blank honestly.

Now write the date you believe that condition will arrive. Be specific. If you cannot write a date, you have your answer: the condition is not a plan. It is an excuse.

Finally, write the sentence that ends the waiting: *"I am not ready. I will never be ready. I am doing it now anyway."*

Then do the next thing you have been waiting to do. The feeling arrives after the act. It has never arrived before it.

# Chapter 17: Health

The toll of perfectionism is not confined to your projects. It is written on your body.

I am not a doctor, and I am not going to pretend to be one. But I have spent years around perfectionists, I was one myself, and I have read enough of the research to know this: the body keeps score. A mind that spends its days braced against a verdict it fears will produce a body that never fully relaxes. And a body that never relaxes pays for it.

Let me begin with the most obvious cost: sleep. The perfectionist does not sleep well. The unfinished work does not clock out at bedtime. In the dark, with the day's distractions gone, the hum returns, and with it the review. The perfectionist lies in bed replaying the day's work, finding the flaws, planning the fix. The mind, which should be winding down, is running a quality-control audit at full volume.

This is not just unpleasant. It is destructive. Sleep is when the body repairs and the mind consolidates. The perfectionist steals hours from it every night, and the deficit compounds. The exhaustion of the sleep-deprived mind is precisely the state in which the perfectionist's judgment is most distorted, the flaws loom largest, the fear is loudest, and the compulsion to polish is strongest. Perfectionism robs you of sleep, and the lost sleep makes the perfectionism worse.

Then there is the tension. Watch a perfectionist work and you will see it: the jaw, the shoulders, the neck, the hands. The body is held in a state of perpetual readiness, as if waiting for a blow. Over months and years, that holding becomes chronic. The neck aches. The shoulders knot. The jaw grinds. The perfectionist visits physiotherapists and dentists and wonders why their body is falling apart, and nobody connects the dots back to the eleven-hour workday spent flinching.

And there is the stress itself. The perfectionist lives in a state of low-grade emergency. The unfinished work is a permanent stressor, and the body cannot tell the difference between a real threat and an imagined one. It floods the system with stress hormones, keeps the heart rate elevated, keeps the immune system distracted. The perfectionist catches every cold, sleeps poorly, eats badly, and blames the office, the weather, the workload, anything but the real culprit: a mind that has been under siege for years.

I also want to speak about the shame, because the shame has a body of its own. The perfectionist carries a permanent sense of falling short. The body knows this. It shows up as the heaviness in the chest, the knot in the stomach, the slumped posture of a person apologizing for existing. The shame is not a feeling you can simply think your way out of. It is lodged in the body, and it is fed daily by the loop: start, fear, polish, never ship, feel the failure, repeat.

Here is the part I want you to hear clearly. **The standard is not protecting your health. The standard is costing you your health.** You have been telling yourself that the standards are the price of excellence, and the tension, the sleeplessness, the shame are the cost of doing the job properly. They are not. They are the cost of doing the job *impossibly*. The person who ships the good-enough report and closes the laptop goes home with an unbraced body. The person who holds the report hostage to the flawless standard goes home tight, exhausted, and sick.

Consider what the perfect work is actually buying you. Is it buying you better health? No. Is it buying you more years? No. Is it buying you the approval you are terrified of losing? Not even that, because the work never ships, so the approval is never tested.

The health costs of perfectionism are real, they are compounding, and they are entirely optional. They are the price of a demand that can never be met. Let the demand go, and the body begins to let go too. The shoulders drop. The jaw unclenches. The sleep (eventually, after the withdrawal) comes back.

You have one body, and it has been running on high alert for years to protect a standard that has never once protected it back. You can keep serving the standard and keep paying in sleep, tension, and stress. Or you can ship the work, close the loop, and give your body the first real rest it has had in years.

**EXERCISE: The Body's Statement**

Put the book down. Close your eyes. Scan your body, slowly, from your feet to your head. Notice every place that is held tight: the jaw, the neck, the shoulders, the stomach, the hands.

For each held place, ask yourself: *What is this bracing against?* The answer, almost always, is the same: the verdict. The unfinished work. The standard that must be met.

Now, for the rest of today, do this experiment: whenever you notice your body bracing, say the answer out loud, even if you feel silly: "I am bracing against an imaginary verdict."

And then let the shoulders drop. Let the jaw soften. The verdict is imaginary. The body can rest.

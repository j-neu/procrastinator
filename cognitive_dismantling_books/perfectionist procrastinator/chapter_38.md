# Chapter 38: Should I Avoid "Judged" Situations?

There is a question the perfectionist asks when the change begins, and the answer to it will determine whether the change survives.

The question is: *"Should I avoid situations where my work will be judged?"*

It is a reasonable question, and the perfectionist asks it with the best intentions. They have learned that the fear of judgment is the engine of the trap. So they reason: if the judgment is the problem, I will avoid the judgment. I will not submit the work. I will keep my imperfect work to myself, and I will grow strong in private, and one day, when I am ready, I will face the world.

The logic is impeccable. The conclusion is fatal.

Here is the truth, and you must understand it before it destroys the change: **the fear of judgment is not cured by avoiding judgment. It is cured by surviving it.**

Think about how fear works. The fear of judgment is a reflex. It fires when you approach the release. Every time you release and survive, the reflex weakens. Every time you avoid the release, the reflex strengthens—because avoidance is the reflex's victory. Each avoidance teaches the fear: *you were right to be scared. We avoided it. We are safe.* The fear grows on the evidence of the avoidance.

The perfectionist who avoids judged situations is not protecting themselves from the fear. They are feeding it. They are keeping the fear alive, in a warm, dark room, where it grows stronger every day, until the day finally comes when they must face a judged situation—because it always comes—and the fear is enormous, because it has been fed for months on avoidance.

Now consider the perfectionist who faces the judgment instead. The first release is terrifying. The second is less terrifying. The third is uncomfortable. The tenth is routine. The fear does not disappear; it shrinks, because every release is evidence that the world does not end.

There is no other way to shrink the fear. You cannot think it smaller. You cannot rest it smaller. You cannot avoid it smaller. The only thing that shrinks the fear of judgment is judgment—survived, repeatedly, until the body learns what the mind already knows: it does not kill.

This is the lesson the perfectionist has been missing their entire life. They have been protecting themselves from the verdict, and the protection is the poison. The verdict they fear has never once killed them. They have been wounded by the anticipation, not by the event. And the anticipation grows in the avoidance. The event, when it finally comes, is almost always milder than the anticipation that preceded it.

Now let me be precise about what I am not saying. I am not telling you to court disaster. I am not telling you to release work that will end your career. The judgment you need to face is not the catastrophic judgment; it is the ordinary judgment. The report that might get a lukewarm response. The piece that might be called "fine." These are the judgments that make up ninety-nine percent of the fear, and they are the ones you have been avoiding.

The catastrophic scenarios—the public humiliation, the professional ruin—these are the fear's fantasies. The judgment you actually face, when you release the work, is ordinary. It is a comment, a grade, a review, a silence. And the silence, by the way, is the most common judgment of all: most work is released into a world that barely notices. The verdict you have been dreading for months is, in the actual event, a shrug.

So the answer to the question is no. Do not avoid judged situations. Do not hide your imperfect work until it is perfect—that is the old trap, with a new name. Release it, into the judgment, and let the judgment teach you what the avoidance never could: that you survive it. That the verdict is not a verdict on your worth.

There is a particular beauty in this. The first time you release imperfect work and receive the ordinary, survivable judgment, you will experience something the perfectionist has never felt: relief. Not the false relief of the sealed work. The real relief of the released work. The relief of the loop closed, the verdict survived, the fear proven wrong. It is a clean feeling, and it is the feeling you have been chasing your whole life without knowing what it was. You have been getting the false version of it, from the polishing. This is the real version, and it comes only through the release.

Do not hide. The judgment is not your enemy. The judgment is your medicine, and you have been refusing to take it for years.

**EXERCISE: The Judgment Ladder**

Put the book down. Take your paper. Write down, in order of increasing fear, the judged situations you have been avoiding. Start with the small ones: a message you are afraid to send, a post you are afraid to publish. End with the large ones: the presentation, the application, the submission.

Now, starting at the bottom of the ladder, release the smallest thing today. Not when it is perfect. Now, as it is.

Tomorrow, release the next one. And the next.

Each release is a dose of medicine. Each survival is a weakening of the fear. By the end of the ladder, you will have faced the judgment you feared most, and you will have discovered the secret the trap has been hiding from you your whole life:

You survive it. You always could. The avoidance was the only thing keeping you small.
